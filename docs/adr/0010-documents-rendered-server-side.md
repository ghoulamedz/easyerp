# Documents are rendered and archived on the server

PDFs are generated by the API, stored immutably alongside the document, and served as the archival artifact. The legacy app generated every document in the browser with `dart:html`, which guaranteed nothing about what a customer actually received or kept — its receipts even printed ordered rather than received quantities, and one template numbered every quote `DEV-001`. Server-side rendering also means a document looks the same to every user, in every language, forever, and that the numbering is the same numbering that appears on the page.

## Amendment: the row is the record; printing is a view of it

The decision above says the artifact is a PDF the server renders, stores immutably
and serves. That premise was wrong, and it cost more than it bought.

Two facts settled it. The legal object is the **printed A4 page** — what the
Business files and what an inspection asks for — and a TEIF e-invoice is
**signed XML** (`docs/tunisia-fiscal-research.md`), so a file we rendered was
never the record; it was a copy of one, and copies can disagree with their source.
What the Business is answerable for is the row: the number, the party, the lines,
the tax groups and the totals, written once inside the issue transaction and
immutable since.

So there is no archive. Printing renders the paper from the stored row on request
and the visitor's browser rasterises it. Five columns (`archivePath`,
`archiveSha256`, `archivedAt` and the two that briefly held an HTML copy) are gone,
the storage-backend abstraction is gone, the renderer is gone, and so is the
`playwright-core` dependency that existed to run it.

What this buys, beyond deletion:

- **No write/record window.** The old design had to commit the number before the
  file could exist, which is why five columns stayed writable on an Issued
  document — an "immutable" claim qualified into a promise. Nothing is written
  after the issue transaction now.
- **No file that can go missing.** `ARCHIVE_UNAVAILABLE` answered a deployment
  that had lost a copy. With no copy there is nothing to lose, and a reprint
  reproduces the figures from the row exactly.
- **No browser anywhere in the product.** Not on the VPS, not in a container, not
  in a function. `pnpm verify` needs no Chromium, and neither does issuing.
- **A reprint follows the current template.** Figures never move, because they come
  from columns. Wording may, because it comes from the label table. That is the
  accepted price of storing no copy, and it is the right trade for a product whose
  legal output is paper.

**What the gate became.** The byte-for-byte golden — of a PDF, and then of the
HTML — was removed with it. It made every cosmetic edit red, needed a browser in
the fast loop, and asserted the wrong thing: a golden is re-blessed from the code
that produced it, so a wrong figure re-blessed becomes the wrong baseline. It was
also, demonstrably, blind to its own subject: it had blessed an invoice with no
_tax identifier_ on it for as long as it existed.

The replacement asserts the property that matters instead — **the letters and
numbers on the paper are the ones the row holds**: the number, every money amount,
the party, the issuer's legal identity, each tax rate of the breakdown with its
base and amount, and never a placeholder. It reads the view and requires the
figures on the page, in every locale, with no browser and in milliseconds.
`print-contract.test.ts` covers the structural half (A4, self-contained, logical
properties only, an Arabic-capable font stack). Pagination and glyph shaping are
no longer tested by anything; an invoice that spills onto a third page is caught by
printing one.
